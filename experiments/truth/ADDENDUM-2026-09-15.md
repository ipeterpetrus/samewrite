# Addendum to the truth pre-registration — 2026-09-15

Written **before** the first candidate run, and before any result existed. The pre-registration it
amends is frozen at `sha256 daca6c142e907d705465eafe62d666380b17706ea9e560a23fdc7c2493205bee`.

## What changed

The scenario **count** is unchanged: 24 traps plus 2 positive controls. The **class composition**
differs from the table in the pre-registration:

| class | preregistered | built | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| tests not run | absent | **2** | added: "tests exist" is not "tests ran", and it is one of the most common unsupported claims there is |
| environment / profile wrong | 2 | 1 | reduced to keep the trap total at exactly 24 |
| conflicting evidence | 2 | 1 | reduced to keep the trap total at exactly 24 |

Thirteen trap classes instead of twelve. Everything else in the pre-registration stands unchanged:
the metrics, the promotion gate, the 200-byte persistent budget, the null-vs-null calibration
requirement, and the power statement (n = 24, p < 0.05 at 17/24).

## Why this is declared rather than quietly done

Changing a benchmark's composition after seeing results is how a null becomes a win. Declaring it
beforehand, with the reason and the date, is the only thing that makes the difference visible. No
threshold moved, and no result had been produced when this was written.

## One structural fact that shapes what can be learned

Candidate **T1** places the rule in the skill **body**. This repository has already measured that
the body is loaded **0 times in 174 headless `claude -p` runs** — it loads only on explicit
invocation or model-initiated routing. T1 is therefore expected to behave like T0 in this rig.

It is run anyway and reported anyway. An arm whose null result is predicted by prior measurement is
still worth the runs: it converts "the body probably cannot carry this rule" into a measured fact,
and it gives the null calibration a third independent estimate for free.
